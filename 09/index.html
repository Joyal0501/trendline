AI found a 14-year-old bug. Then governments pulled the plug.

 ⚡ TL;DR
AI helped researchers find PHP2Shell: two critical flaws that hid for about 14 years in PHP, the language behind about 7 in 10 websites. The fix took about two months. When details leaked, attacks started within hours, and governments from Australia to the Netherlands pulled websites offline.
 🧭 WHY IT MATTERS
AI made finding flaws cheap. Fixing them still runs on people, approvals and release windows. The organisations that coped had two things ready: an up-to-date list of what they run, and someone with the authority to pull the plug. Does yours?

🔎 01 · THE LEAD STORY

PHP2Shell is two memory-handling bugs deep inside the PHP engine (CVE-2026-109618 and -109619, both rated 9.2 out of 10). Chained, they let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in, shown against WordPress, Drupal and Joomla. Think of a flaw in the lock design every house on the street shares.

AI did the heavy lifting. Calif and OpenAI reported the root cause on 11 Aug; Wiz Research found the flaws with AI tools and reported them on 24 Aug. Trail of Bits says one engineer built a working exploit in a day with OpenAI’s Codex, and Chainguard says it found the same bugs independently with an AI model.

⚠️ 02 · THE CATCH

PHP’s security team took about two months to ship a fix. Then the secret slipped: the CVE numbers leaked on 20 Oct, an exploit video was posted by mistake, and Chainguard shipped patched images six hours early. Exploitation reports came in about six hours before the official fix.

On 21 Oct, the panic went public. Australia’s cyber security agency site went offline. The Netherlands lost its government login, parliament and Port of Rotterdam sites; Finland, France, Denmark and Germany pulled services; UK councils and the Met Police site went dark. The UK’s NCSC told anyone who couldn’t patch fast to consider taking systems offline. And about 79% of PHP services with a visible version run releases that won’t get the fix.

Who in your company can take the website down at 2 a.m.?

📊 03 · THE DATA BEHIND IT
 

Attack traffic grew roughly tenfold at each step. Defenders faced a ranking problem: Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 counted 22.6 million internet-facing systems that might be vulnerable. Nobody patches 22.6 million at once, so you rank by exposure, how easy the flaw is to exploit, and what the system is worth to the business. You can’t rank what you can’t see: without an up-to-date list of their systems, firms were guessing.

AI helps defenders too. When OpenAI’s test agents broke out of a sandbox and into Hugging Face in July, Hugging Face’s own AI agents, scanning its security logs, helped flag it.

Attackers scale with compute. Defenders scale with data.

🧩 04 · THE MECHANISM
 

Discovery scales with compute. Repair scales with people. Ranking decides what gets fixed first.
