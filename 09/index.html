TL;DR
 
A flaw that sat unnoticed in PHP for about 14 years was found with AI. PHP runs about 7 in 10 websites.
 
Fixing it took two months. Attacks started before the fix was out.
 
Governments from Australia to the Netherlands took their own websites offline.
Why you should care

Every company runs old code nobody has looked at in years. AI now finds the holes in it in days, for attackers and defenders alike. So the real test isn’t spotting a risk. It’s whether the company knows what it runs and how fast it can act.

01The lead story

PHP2Shell lets an attacker take over a web server without logging in, including sites built on WordPress, Drupal and Joomla. Three separate teams (Calif, OpenAI and Wiz) found it with AI help in August, and one engineer turned it into a working attack in a day.

02The catch

Finding was fast; fixing wasn’t. The patch took about two months, the bug IDs leaked a day early, and attacks were reported before the fix shipped. Then the panic: Australia’s cyber agency, Dutch government logins and UK councils all went offline. About 79% of PHP servers that show their version run old releases that won’t get the fix at all.

03Wait, what?
1Physician, heal thyself. Australia’s national cyber security website was among the sites knocked offline.
2The oops video. A research team posted a clip of its exploit by mistake the night before the fix.
3Attackers were early. Amazon’s decoy servers logged an attack attempt four days before the fix.
04The data

Attack attempts grew about tenfold at each step. With 22.6 million possibly exposed systems, the hard part for defenders was ranking, not finding.

05The mechanism

“Discovery scales with compute. Repair scales with people. Ranking decides what gets fixed first.”

06The real lesson

Rank by risk, then patch. Prioritisation wins.

Ask your team: who can take our website down at 2 a.m.?
