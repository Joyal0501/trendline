AI found a 14-year-old bug. Then governments pulled the plug.

 
⚡ TL;DR

AI helped researchers find two critical flaws that sat in PHP for about 14 years. PHP runs about 7 in 10 websites. The fix took two months, and when details leaked, governments from Australia to the Netherlands took their websites offline.

 
🎓 Why you should care

AI made finding flaws cheap. Fixing them still runs on people, approvals and release windows. The organisations that coped knew what they ran and who could pull the plug. Does yours?

01The lead story

PHP2Shell lets an attacker take over a web server without logging in, including sites built on WordPress, Drupal and Joomla. Three separate teams (Calif, OpenAI and Wiz) found it with AI help in August, and one engineer turned it into a working attack in a day.

02The catch

Finding was fast; fixing wasn’t. The patch took about two months, the bug IDs leaked a day early, and attacks were reported before the fix shipped. Then the panic: Australia’s cyber agency, Dutch government logins and UK councils all went offline. About 79% of PHP servers that show their version run old releases that won’t get the fix at all.

03Wait, what?
🙃Physician, heal thyself. Australia’s national cyber security website was among the sites knocked offline.
🎬The oops video. A research team posted a clip of its exploit by mistake the night before the fix.
🍯Attackers were early. Amazon’s decoy servers logged an attack attempt four days before the fix.
04The data

Attack attempts grew about tenfold at each step. With 22.6 million possibly exposed systems, the hard part for defenders was ranking, not finding.

05The mechanism

Discovery scales with compute. Repair scales with people. Ranking decides what gets fixed first.
